# Cinder Instagram + Facebook Outreach Playbook

Status: operating procedure for founder-reviewed, manually executed outreach.
Scope: Instagram and Facebook only. Cinder prepares and tracks work; it does not send messages, follow/unfollow, comment, scrape, or automate account activity.

## Goal

Create more qualified conversations with business owners by being relevant, specific, respectful, and consistent—not by maximizing message volume. The primary conversion is a real conversation or permission to share a useful observation, not an immediate sale.

## Prospect qualification

Before drafting a message, Cinder should capture:
- Business name, website, location, niche, Instagram URL, Facebook Page URL, and source.
- A dated observation from a public business profile or website, with the source URL.
- One concrete business-relevant reason to contact them (for example, unclear booking path, unanswered public questions, weak lead capture, or a visible gap between an offer and its next step).
- Confidence and evidence. Keep hypotheses labelled as hypotheses; never invent missed calls, lost revenue, ad spend, or operational failures.
- A clear channel and next action. If the profile is private, the owner is unclear, or no relevant angle exists, skip or research further.

## Manual daily workflow

1. Review a small, manageable batch of qualified businesses in Cinder's outreach queue.
2. Open the business's public Instagram or Facebook Page from the contact record; verify it is the correct business and that the observation is still current.
3. Read recent public content and existing conversations where available. Do not repeatedly comment, mass-follow, or use fake engagement to get noticed.
4. Use a short, individually edited opening message. Mention one true, specific observation and ask one easy-to-answer question. Avoid links, attachments, and a pitch dump in the first touch.
5. The founder manually sends the message inside the official app/site. Cinder must not claim a message was sent until the founder logs that outcome.
6. Log the channel, timestamp, exact outcome, and a brief note in Cinder. Set a realistic next action.
7. If they reply, pause the follow-up sequence immediately and handle the conversation as a human. If they decline, ask not to be contacted, or otherwise opt out, mark do-not-contact and stop outreach across channels.
8. Follow up only where appropriate and permitted by platform rules. If there is no response, use at most one brief, context-aware follow-up after a reasonable interval; then stop. Do not cycle through alternate accounts or channels to bypass silence or a decline.
9. Review weekly: messages sent (manually logged), positive replies, qualified conversations, meetings booked, meetings held, and deals won. Improve targeting and relevance before increasing volume.

## Drafting framework

**First touch**
- Personal observation: one verifiable detail about their service, offer, booking path, or recent public post.
- Context: why that detail prompted the outreach.
- Question: one low-pressure question that invites a reply.
- No false familiarity, exaggerated praise, unsupported claims, fake urgency, or guarantee of results.

**Example structure (rewrite for each prospect; do not send verbatim at scale)**

> Hey [name/business] — I noticed [specific, verifiable observation]. I work with service businesses on [relevant revenue/lead-handling problem], and I was curious: how are you currently handling [one relevant process]?

Only use the example if the claimed work and observation are true. If no genuine observation can be found, do not send a message.

**Follow-up**
- Refer to the original context in one sentence.
- Add a useful detail or ask a simpler version of the question.
- Make it easy to ignore or decline.
- One follow-up maximum by default; no reply means stop.

**When someone replies**
- Answer their actual question before pitching.
- Ask permission before sending an audit, demo, or additional material.
- If they express interest, agree on one next step and record it.
- If they are not interested, acknowledge politely and close the loop.

## Account and platform safety

- Use official Instagram/Facebook apps and approved platform features.
- No bots, browser automation, scraping, mass-DM tools, fake accounts, purchased engagement, credential sharing, or attempts to evade rate limits.
- No fixed “safe” DM quota is promised: platform limits and enforcement vary. Keep activity human-paced and follow current Meta rules and account notices.
- Do not send identical messages in bulk. Personalization must reflect real evidence, not superficial name insertion.
- Respect privacy settings, refusals, blocks, and opt-outs immediately.
- Do not move to SMS unless the person has explicitly consented to text communication. Never draft cold SMS.
- Do not make claims about a business's missed leads, revenue, or internal process without evidence.
- Never store passwords, session cookies, access tokens, or private customer data in Cinder notes.

## Cinder workflow requirements

Each social prospect record should support:
- Clickable Instagram and Facebook profile URLs.
- Source/evidence notes and research date.
- Draft message stored as a draft, not a sent event.
- Manual activity logging for sent, reply, positive reply, not interested, meeting booked, and opt-out.
- Sequence state: ready, drafted, manually contacted, awaiting reply, reply needs review, meeting booked, closed, or suppressed.
- Replies and opt-outs pause/stop follow-up progression; suppressed contacts cannot be re-enrolled.
- A visible next action and due date; no silent background sending.
- Reporting that distinguishes drafted messages from manually confirmed sends.

## Success metrics

Track weekly:
- Qualified prospects reviewed.
- Personalized drafts prepared.
- Messages manually confirmed as sent.
- Replies and positive replies.
- Qualified conversations and meetings booked/held.
- Deals won and contracted value attributed to the channel.

Use small-sample results cautiously. Optimize the quality of targeting, observation, and offer before raising activity volume.
