# Gaps against the big CRMs, November 16 2026

Source: a feature inventory of Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz, GoHighLevel, JobNimbus, AccuLynx,
ServiceM8, FieldEdge, Kickserv, Markate and mHelpDesk (about 200 features), checked against Cinder's code. The goal
is that a contractor runs the whole business here and nothing else.

## Already in Cinder (the table stakes we have)

Online booking and request forms; a lead inbox for every source with instant replies; missed-call text-back; the AI
agent answering texts and booking; a lead-to-paid pipeline; quotes from a price list with options, deposits,
e-sign and automatic follow-ups; financing by link; change orders; a drag-and-drop calendar, a crew dispatch view
and per-person routes; on-my-way texts; service plans with visit reminders and invoices; multi-day installs; the
crew app with checklists, photos, sign-offs, a time clock and offline mode; job costing (entered by hand); property
profiles; invoices with reminders and a pay link; a customer page; appointment reminders; two-way texting and
email; review requests; win-back texts; QuickBooks sync; reports for revenue, profit, lead sources (to what was
paid) and receivables aging; roles, and crew limited to their jobs; import from five tools with undo; export
everything.

## Gaps we build (in this order)

### Batch A: money and the back office (every contractor, every week)
1. Expenses and receipts: snap a receipt, pick the job, it counts in job costing. Mileage log at a set rate.
2. Progress invoicing: bill part of a job (a percentage or an amount) and the rest later.
3. Batch invoicing: every finished job that isn't invoiced, in one pass.
4. Customer statements: everything a customer owes, one link.
5. Late fees: an optional fee on invoices past due, added once, owner's choice.
6. Timesheets and payroll export: hours by person by week, at their rates, as a file for the payroll service.

### Batch B: jobs and what's at each address
7. Equipment per property: make, model, serial, installed date, warranty end, service history (HVAC, plumbing,
   pools, generators).
8. Job documents: permits, contracts, drawings, PDFs kept with the job.
9. Office tasks: to-dos with a due date and an owner, beyond per-customer reminders.

### Batch C: customers and marketing
10. Customer tags and segments, and a text or email campaign to a segment (consent-aware).
11. Referrals: who referred whom, and a thank-you when the job is won.
12. Feedback after the job: a one-tap rating; anything low alerts the owner. Review requests still go to everyone
    (asking only happy customers breaks Google's rules).
13. Photo report to the customer when the job is done.

### Batch D: reports and nudges
14. Revenue goal for the month on Today.
15. Job profit alert when costs eat the margin.
16. Crew performance: revenue, jobs and hours by person.
17. Sales tax report.

## Gaps we don't build now (need outside accounts, contracts or a policy decision)

- Card processing, tap to pay, ACH, card on file, autopay, instant payouts, tipping: by design, customers pay
  through the owner's own pay link and Cinder never handles card details.
- VoIP phone system, call recording, call tracking numbers per ad: Twilio could do it; call recording needs consent
  handling by state. A decision for Sam.
- AI phone receptionist: out of scope by Sam's instruction (no voice answering).
- Live GPS of techs and fleet telematics, live "track my arrival" link: needs background location on the crew's
  phones (a native app), not a web app.
- Supplier integrations and material ordering (ABC Supply, SRS, Home Depot), aerial roof measurements (EagleView,
  Hover): partner agreements.
- Native Facebook lead ads (Meta app review), Google LSA API (partner access), Xero sync.
- Website builder, ads manager, social scheduler, postcards: marketing products of their own.
- Automation builder with custom triggers: the common automations are built in; a builder is a later project.
- Multi-location and franchise.
